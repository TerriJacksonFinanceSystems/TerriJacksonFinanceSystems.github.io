This case study explores how an interactive FP&A dashboard can support the monthly management-reporting cycle by helping Finance move from validated financial data through variance analysis, business challenge and management decision-making.

01The Finance Problem Statement

Monthly management reporting can consume significant FP&A capacity before meaningful analysis begins. Finance teams can often spend substantial time extracting and preparing data, updating recurring reports, checking calculations and formatting outputs for management review.

Where these activities rely on manual manipulation or repetitive report production, they can also introduce human error. Identifying, investigating and correcting those errors creates further rework and can reduce confidence in the final reporting output.

The combined effect can create a time lag between period close and meaningful management decisions. Time spent producing and correcting reports is time that is not available for investigating material movements, understanding business drivers, challenging assumptions and explanations, assessing forecast implications and supporting management decisions.

This prototype explores whether a more automated, interactive reporting approach could shorten that path from financial close to decision-useful insight. The dashboard is designed to process a controlled monthly dataset, perform repeatable calculations and visualisations, and make relevant movements easier for FP&A to identify and investigate.

Automation alone, however, does not solve the finance problem. Faster report production is only valuable if the resulting information remains accurate, reconciled, appropriately controlled and subject to human financial judgement.

How can an interactive FP&A dashboard reduce recurring report-production effort and accelerate the move from period close to meaningful analysis, while preserving financial reconciliation, clear accountability and human judgement?

The objective is not to automate the FP&A decision process. It is to reduce repetitive reporting effort and provide a faster, controlled starting point for Finance to investigate drivers, challenge explanations, support management decisions and ultimately create more capacity for the 'A' in FP&A.

02The Prototype Objective

The prototype was designed to test a practical approach to YoY cost-centre expense reporting and variance analysis using a lightweight, self-contained interactive dashboard.

  • load a controlled monthly dataset
  • analyse current-year and prior-year cost performance
  • identify material year-on-year movements
  • investigate cost-centre, category and GL-level drivers
  • use interactive visualisations to support analysis
  • develop an initial management story from the financial movements
  • carry that analysis into business challenge, management review and action

The dashboard is deliberately positioned within an end-to-end FP&A process, rather than as a standalone reporting application.

Benefit hypothesis: By reducing repetitive report production and providing repeatable calculations and visualisations from a controlled dataset, the prototype explores whether FP&A could reach reconciled analysis sooner, contribute to shorter close-to-decision turnaround, and redirect capacity from report production and rework towards analysis, challenge and decision support.

03Intended Users & Decision Questions

The primary intended user is an FP&A Manager or Finance Business Partner conducting monthly cost and variance analysis. The wider process also involves cost-centre budget holders, General Ledger / R2R, FP&A leadership and CFO / Finance leadership.

  • Where have costs moved materially compared with the prior year?
  • Which cost centres, categories or GL accounts are driving the movement?
  • Which movements warrant further investigation?
  • What questions should FP&A take to the relevant budget holder?
  • Is the movement temporary, timing-related, one-off or indicative of a change in the underlying run-rate?
  • What financial or operational implications should be reflected in the management story?
  • What management action, escalation or forecast response might be required?
The dashboard supports the analysis informing these decisions; it does not make them. Operational explanations, financial interpretation, forecast implications and management actions remain subject to human judgement and appropriate organisational accountability.

05End-to-End FP&A Process

Close & Release→Prepare Data→Load & Check→Reconcile & Analyse→Explain & Challenge→Finalise Story→Review & Decide→Follow Through

The process begins with the controlled financial close and release of approved source data - not with the dashboard itself.

FP&A then prepares and validates the monthly source file before upload. The dashboard performs technical validation and calculates the selected management views, after which FP&A reconciles the resulting output to approved source controls. Only after that reconciliation does the process move into interpretation and investigation.

Material movements are then discussed with the relevant cost-centre budget holder, challenged by FP&A and incorporated into a management story covering performance, risk, forecast implications and potential decisions.

Finance leadership reviews the management story, challenges priorities and determines appropriate action. Decisions, actions and any resulting forecast updates are then carried forward into the next reporting cycle.

This creates a closed management loop, rather than treating production of the dashboard or management pack as the end of the FP&A process.

06Process Ownership & RACI Matrix

A detailed process definition supports the high-level workflow.

  • process phase and lane
  • activity
  • primary output or control
  • whether the activity represents human activity, human judgement, human control, human approval/decision, or automated processing/control
  • the boundary of the current prototype
  • Responsible, Accountable, Consulted and Informed roles

The RACI overlay is intended to make ownership and hand-offs explicit across Finance, budget holders, technical support and leadership.

This separates where and how an activity is performed from who remains responsible and accountable for it.

For example, the HTML dashboard can execute validation and calculations, but responsibility and accountability for the finance process remain with defined human roles. Similarly, management decisions and approvals sit outside automated decision-making.

07Data, Assumptions & Method

The prototype uses fully synthetic data created for portfolio demonstration purposes. No employer, client or restricted training-provider data is used.

Data

The monthly CSV contains the financial dimensions and measures required by the dashboard. Core fields include Department/Cost Centre, Cost Category, Current-Year Cost and Prior-Year Cost. Optional fields can enrich the analysis with additional GL/account, category and headcount analysis.

The dashboard validates required fields and parses usable numeric records before calculating the selected reporting views. The prototype is currently self-contained: the uploaded file remains local to the browser and no live ERP or EPM extraction or integration is demonstrated.

Analytical Method

The dashboard compares current-year and prior-year cost data and calculates absolute £ and % YoY movements across the selected reporting view.

Interactive filters and analytical views allow FP&A to examine movements from different perspectives, including department/cost centre, cost category and, where the relevant optional data is available, GL/account and headcount-related analysis.

The resulting calculations and visualisations are intended to identify and prioritise areas for investigation. They do not, by themselves, establish the cause, commercial significance or appropriate management response to a variance.

From Analysis to Judgement

The dashboard provides a repeatable analytical starting point, not a substitute for FP&A judgement.

Data validation→Calculation & visualisation→Finance reconciliation→Investigation→Operational explanation→FP&A challenge→Management decision

This distinction is important because technically valid data can still be financially incomplete or incorrectly classified, and a correctly calculated variance does not explain why the movement occurred or what should be done about it.

08Controls & Human Oversight

Technical validity is not the same as accounting accuracy.

Successfully loading and processing a file demonstrates that the data is technically usable; it does not demonstrate that the financial information is complete, accurate or reconciled.

The process therefore incorporates human finance controls both before and after dashboard processing.

Before upload · preventative controlFP&A checks completeness, mappings, sign convention, row counts and control totals against the approved source before preparing the controlled monthly CSV.
After processing · reconciliation controlFP&A reconciles loaded row counts, totals and representative detailed records to approved source controls before relying on the dashboard for management analysis.
FP&A identifies→Budget holders explain→FP&A challenges→Finance leadership decides→FP&A follows through

The dashboard therefore supports financial analysis without replacing accounting controls, commercial judgement or management accountability.

09Prototype Boundaries & Limitations

This is a portfolio prototype developed for applied learning and demonstration rather than a production finance application.

  • live ERP/EPM integration or automated source-data extraction
  • production-grade authentication or role-based access controls
  • automated workflow or formal management-pack approval
  • integrated forecast updates or workflow-based action tracking
  • autonomous financial interpretation or decision-making
  • production deployment, scalability or operational support arrangements
  • measured productivity improvements, financial benefits or ROI

The prototype should therefore be evaluated as evidence of the finance use case, analytical approach, process design, control thinking and prototype development approach, rather than as evidence of a production implementation.

10From Prototype to Production

The prototype demonstrates a lightweight approach that could be developed incrementally rather than requiring a large-scale implementation from the outset.

The next steps would depend on the intended use, existing finance systems and level of automation required. Priorities could include:

  • connecting to approved finance data sources
  • defining appropriate access, ownership and reconciliation controls
  • testing the solution with users and representative finance scenarios
  • documenting the process and providing appropriate user support
  • measuring whether the solution reduces reporting effort and shortens the path from close to analysis and decision support

A practical implementation could therefore begin with a focused use case and controlled pilot, with integration, automation and governance developed further as the value and requirements become clearer.

Future AI enhancement: Generative AI could also be explored for activities such as draft variance commentary, investigation prompts or management-summary support, with appropriate human review and traceability to approved financial data.
